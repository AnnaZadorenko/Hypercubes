A 3-cube is the familiar three-dimensional version of a hypercube. This animation shows how it can be built up from a point that moves to give a line, with the line then moving perpendicular to itself to give a square and the square moving perpendicular to itself to give the cube. The animation shows how this constuction appears when it projected on to the two-dimensional plane of the computer screen.     
This construction is generalized to higher dimensions in the succeeding displays. The number of vertices of an n-cube is V(n) = 2^{n} and the number of edges is E(n) = n x 2^{n-1}. A cube, with n = 3, has 8 vertices and 6 edges.
