A 3-cube is the familiar three-dimensional version of a hypercube.  It can be obtained from a 2-cube (or square) by moving the square perpendicular to itself along the third dimension and joining each of the new vertices to the old one from which it arose. The number of vertices of a n-cube is V(n) = 2^{n} and the number of its edges is E(n) = n x 2^{n-1}. A cube, with n = 3, has 8 vertices and 6 edges.
